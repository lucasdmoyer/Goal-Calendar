# Design: more room for life

## Purpose

Success means a person can trust that their ideas are held, feel available to people around them, and maintain a few health-supporting routines through ordinary busy weeks. The app should take less attention than it saves.

## Three layers

1. Values are continuing directions, such as health, presence, relationships, and curiosity.
2. Active discretionary goals are deliberately chosen projects with a reason and a concrete next action. Start with a maximum of two; this is a product choice, not a clinical rule.
3. Inbox and parked ideas are preserved possibilities, without invented deadlines or automatic reminders. Historical suggestions remain suggestions until the user confirms them.

Work obligations, ongoing care, and household responsibilities still matter. The two-goal limit is not permission to ignore them. Actual time-sensitive commitments must stay visible even when capacity is low.

## Today

Ask how much room is available and what real commitments need accounting for. Choose at most one optional next action from active goals. Fit both effort and duration. If none fits, suggest no optional action. A Smaller action must be explicitly a limited planning step, not a claim that a large task can be completed faster. A completed action does not automatically generate more work. Closing a day is the user's boundary, not a declaration that all their obligations are complete.

Daily health and relationship defaults should be chosen and editable. A shared activity is an invitation. Do not turn a partner into a monitored subject or require them to use the app.

## Weekly reset

Spend about 15 minutes on what felt good, what felt crowded, real commitments, inbox decisions, one main focus and possibly a second, and one thing to stop or simplify. Treat a missed week as useful information about the system's fit.

## Social availability

Before entering a social setting, capture a recurring thought and decide when to revisit it. Notice something shared, ask one sincere question, and follow the other person's interest. Respect short replies and let a conversation end comfortably. There is no quota or social score.

## Evidence and its limits

- Masicampo and Baumeister (2011) found reduced interference from unfinished goals after participants made specific plans in laboratory studies. This motivates credible next actions and review times; it does not establish that using an app eliminates worry. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688924/
- Epley and Schroeder (2014) found more positive experiences from connecting with strangers than participants expected in commuter experiments. The result is context-specific and does not mean every person wants a conversation. https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/jschroeder/Publications/Epley%26Schroeder2014.pdf
- General sleep and activity guidance belongs in educational context, not an automatic personalized prescription. https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/ and https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html

## Coaching and memory

Version one uses an explainable rules engine and a manually copied brief. It does not have access to previous conversations, a live language model, medical records, calendar events, or personal accounts. The brief distinguishes user-entered data from instructions. A future language model should propose changes to explicit, editable memory, not fabricate historical patterns or infer a diagnosis.

## Deliberate limits

No streaks, life scores, shame about parked ideas, automatically filled empty time, involuntary escalation after missed habits, or automatic activation of new goals. No medical or financial decisions on a person's behalf. Keep notifications opt-in if they are added. Separate each person's private records from voluntarily shared couple commitments.
